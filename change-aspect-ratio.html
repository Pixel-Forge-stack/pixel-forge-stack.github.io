The Basics

What Changing the Aspect Ratio Does

The aspect ratio is the proportion of width to height, for example 1:1, 4:3 or 16:9. Changing it without distorting the picture means either adding space around the image or cutting part of it away.

This tool builds a new canvas at the ratio you choose, based on the image's own pixel size: it extends the shorter side or crops, so the image is never scaled up beyond its original resolution. You can use the presets (1:1, 16:9, 9:16, 4:3, 3:4, 3:2, 2:3, 5:4, 21:9 and 2:1) or enter a custom ratio.

Fill Modes

Choosing How the Extra Space Is Handled

Blur Fill places the original in the centre and fills the rest with a blurred, enlarged copy of the picture.Black Bars and White Bars fill the extra space with a solid colour.Crop to Fit keeps the centre of the picture and cuts the overflow, so nothing is added but part of the image is lost. For control over exactly which area is kept, use Crop Image.

The output keeps the original resolution rather than a fixed size. To end at a specific size, follow up with Resize Image.

Choosing a Ratio

Common Ratios

1:1 is square. 4:3 and 3:2 are common in cameras. 16:9 is the widescreen shape of most monitors and video, and 9:16 is its vertical version. Platforms recommend particular shapes for posts, banners and profile images, and those recommendations change, so check the platform's current guidance.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Does changing the ratio stretch the image? ▾
No. The picture is kept in proportion. The tool either adds a border or blurred background, or crops.
Which fill mode should I choose? ▾
Blur Fill is good for photos going into a different shape, bars for a clean look, and Crop to Fit when you do not want any added space and can lose the edges.
Can I set a custom ratio? ▾
Yes. Enter a width and a height, for example 7 and 5.
What size is the result? ▾
It follows the original image's resolution, so the output dimensions depend on the image and the ratio. The status message shows the exact pixel size after download.
