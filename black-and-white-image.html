What It Does

Converting a Photo to Black and White

This tool turns a colour image into grayscale. Four conversion methods are available, and you can adjust brightness and contrast with sliders while watching the preview.

Luminance-weighted (the default) weights red, green and blue by how bright the eye perceives them, which usually looks the most natural. Simple average treats the channels equally. Brightest channel lifts shadows for a lighter, punchier result, and darkest channel deepens them for a moodier look.
Output

What You Get

The file keeps the type of your original. A JPG source is saved as a JPG and other formats such as PNG, WebP, GIF or BMP are saved as PNG. The file is named with a "-bw" suffix and your original is not changed.

Converting to grayscale removes colour information. It does not fix poor exposure or noise, so adjust brightness and contrast to taste. Keep the colour original in case you want it back. Metadata is normally not carried over to the new file.

Tips

Getting a Good Result

Try each method on your own photo, because the best one depends on the colours in it. Increasing contrast slightly often gives a black-and-white image more depth. For a smaller file, follow up with Compress Image.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is black and white the same as grayscale? ▾
In everyday use, yes: colour is removed and shades of gray remain. Strict black and white with only two tones is a different effect that this tool does not produce.
Which conversion method looks best? ▾
Luminance-weighted is a good default. The others are creative alternatives, so compare them in the preview.
Will the file format change? ▾
JPG sources stay JPG. Other formats are saved as PNG.
Can I convert it back to colour? ▾
No. Keep your original colour image.
