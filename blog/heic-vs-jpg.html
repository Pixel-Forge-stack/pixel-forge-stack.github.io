HEIC vs JPG: Differences, Compatibility and Converting

Why iPhone photos sometimes will not open elsewhere, and what changes when you convert them.

iPhones and iPads save photos as HEIC by default. JPG is older and far more widely supported. Here is how they differ, and what to expect when you convert one to the other.

The Short Comparison

Why Compatibility Differs

HEIC support depends on the operating system, the app and sometimes extra codecs. Apple devices handle it well. Windows may need extra extensions, and many websites, forms and older programs do not accept it. JPG is accepted almost everywhere you upload a photo, which is why people convert.

What Changes When You Convert to JPG

  • Quality: JPG is lossy, so the photo is encoded again. At high quality the difference is small, but it is not an exact copy.
  • File size: the JPG is often larger than the HEIC. Use Compress Image or Resize Image if you need it smaller.
  • Metadata: the HEIC to JPG tool redraws the photo on a canvas, so details such as EXIF and location are normally not carried over.
  • Extras: things stored alongside the picture in HEIC, such as depth data, are not included in the JPG.

Using the Converter

The HEIC to JPG tool relies on your browser to open the HEIC file, so it only works where the browser can decode HEIC. Safari on an iPhone or Mac generally can. If your browser cannot, you will see an error instead of a download.

To avoid conversions in future, an iPhone can save new photos as JPG: open Settings, then Camera, then Formats, and choose Most Compatible. The trade-off is larger files and the loss of HEIC's space savings.

Which Should You Keep?

Keep HEIC as the master if you stay in an Apple environment and want to save space. Use JPG for sharing, uploading to forms and anything that needs to open on many devices. Keep the original in either case before you convert. For a comparison with other web formats, see JPG vs PNG vs WebP.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is HEIC better than JPG? ▾
HEIC is generally more space-efficient at similar visual quality, but JPG is more widely supported. "Better" depends on whether you need compatibility or storage savings.
Does converting HEIC to JPG lose quality? ▾
Slightly, because JPG is lossy. At a high quality setting the difference is usually small.
Why will the converter not open my HEIC file? ▾
The tool depends on your browser decoding HEIC. If it cannot, try Safari on an Apple device, or change the iPhone camera format.
Will the JPG keep my location data? ▾
Do not count on it. The conversion normally drops metadata.
