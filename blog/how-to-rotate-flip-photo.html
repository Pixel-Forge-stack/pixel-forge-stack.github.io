How to Rotate and Flip a Photo the Right Way

Why photos land sideways in the first place, what actually separates rotating from flipping, and how to fix either one without any editing skill required.

You open a photo on your computer and it's lying on its side, even though it looked perfectly upright on your phone. Or a selfie looks mirror flipped compared to how you remember taking it. Both of these are extremely common, both take about ten seconds to fix, and neither one is a sign that anything is actually wrong with the photo file.

Why Photos End Up Sideways

Most phones and cameras don't actually rotate the image data when you turn the device to take a photo. Instead, they save a small piece of information inside the file called orientation metadata, which tells compatible apps and screens which way is up. Your phone reads that metadata and displays the photo correctly. Some other apps, websites, and older software ignore it, and the photo shows up sideways or upside down as a result.

This is why the exact same photo file can look perfectly upright in one app and sideways in another, without the image itself having actually changed at all.

Rotating vs Flipping: What's the Difference

ActionWhat HappensCommon Use
RotateTurns the whole image around its centre by a set angle, usually 90, 180, or 270 degreesFixing a sideways or upside-down photo
FlipCreates a mirror image, left-right or top-to-bottomCorrecting a mirrored selfie or reversing a design element

Rotating spins the photo like turning a printed page. Flipping mirrors it, like looking at it in a mirror rather than turning it. They solve two completely different problems, even though people sometimes use the words interchangeably.

A quick way to tell which one you need: if the photo is sideways or upside down, you need to rotate it. If the photo looks correctly oriented but reversed, like text reading backwards or a selfie that looks "wrong" compared to how you remember the scene, you need to flip it.

Why Selfies Often Look Mirrored

Front-facing phone cameras commonly show a mirrored preview while you're taking the photo, since that feels more natural to look at, similar to a mirror. Some phones save the final photo mirrored to match that preview, while others save it the way a person looking at you would actually see it. Neither is wrong, but it does explain why a selfie sometimes looks like a different photo than what you remember seeing on screen while taking it. Flipping the image horizontally switches between the two.

How to Rotate or Flip a Photo, Step by Step

  1. Upload your photo to a rotate and flip tool by clicking upload or dragging the file onto the page.
  2. Choose rotate if the photo is sideways or upside down, and pick 90 degrees left, 90 degrees right, or 180 degrees depending on how far off it is.
  3. Choose flip instead if the photo needs to be mirrored horizontally or vertically.
  4. Preview the result to confirm it now looks correctly oriented.
  5. Download the corrected photo, ready to use immediately.

Common Mistakes People Make

Rotating when flipping was actually needed, or the other way round. These fix different problems, so applying the wrong one can leave a photo still looking off, or even more confusing than before. Check whether the issue is orientation, meaning rotate, or mirroring, meaning flip.

Rotating 90 degrees the wrong direction. It's an easy slip to rotate left when right was needed. Always preview before downloading rather than assuming the direction was correct.

Not noticing text is mirrored. A flipped photo containing any text, like a sign or a t-shirt logo, will show that text backwards. If a photo needs both a rotate and readable text, check that a flip hasn't accidentally reversed something that should stay readable.

Assuming the file itself is broken. A sideways photo almost never means the file is damaged. It usually just means the orientation metadata isn't being read correctly by whatever app or website is displaying it. Rotating it manually solves this completely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my photo look correct on my phone but sideways everywhere else? ▾
Your phone reads a small piece of orientation data saved inside the photo file and displays it correctly based on that. Some other apps, websites, or older software don't read this data the same way, which makes the same file appear sideways elsewhere, even though nothing about the actual photo has changed.
Does rotating a photo reduce its quality? ▾
Rotating by 90, 180, or 270 degrees doesn't reduce quality, since the pixels are simply rearranged rather than recalculated. Saving the result as a lossless format like PNG keeps it exactly as sharp as the original. Saving as JPG applies a very small amount of standard compression, generally not noticeable.
Can I flip a photo horizontally and vertically at the same time? ▾
Yes, most tools let you apply a horizontal flip and a vertical flip to the same image, which together is the same as rotating the photo 180 degrees. You can also combine a flip with a rotation for more specific orientation needs.
Why do some selfies look different from what I saw while taking them? ▾
Front-facing cameras often show a mirrored live preview since it feels more natural to look at while taking the photo, but the final saved image isn't always mirrored the same way. This mismatch between the preview and the saved file is why the result can look different from what you expected.
