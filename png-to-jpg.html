The Formats

PNG and JPG: What Is the Difference?

PNG is lossless and supports transparency. It works well for screenshots, logos and graphics with sharp edges or flat colours.JPG is lossy and has no transparency. It is designed for photographs and usually produces much smaller files for them.
What Changes

What Happens When You Convert PNG to JPG

Transparency. JPG cannot store it, so this tool fills transparent areas with white before saving. If you need another background colour, add it in an editor first.Quality. JPG is lossy. The slider runs from 10% to 100% and starts at 90%. Higher values keep more detail and produce larger files. Text, thin lines and flat-colour graphics can show fuzzy edges around sharp transitions, so keep the original PNG.File size. For photographs, JPG is usually much smaller than PNG. For simple graphics with few colours, a PNG can be smaller than a JPG, so conversion does not always save space. Compare the two files.Metadata. The JPG is created from the pixels on a canvas, so metadata is normally not carried over.
When To Convert

When JPG Is the Better Choice

Convert when a form or platform asks for JPG, when you want a smaller photo, or when an image has no transparency that you need. Keep PNG for logos, icons and screenshots with text. If the result is still too large, use Compress Image or Resize Image. To go the other way, see JPG to PNG.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to a transparent PNG? ▾
The transparent areas become white, because JPG cannot store transparency.
What quality should I choose? ▾
Around 80 to 90% is a common starting point for photographs. Lower values make smaller files but show more compression artifacts.
Will the JPG always be smaller? ▾
Usually for photographs, but not always for simple graphics. Compare the file sizes.
Can I convert many PNGs at once? ▾
No, one image is converted at a time.
