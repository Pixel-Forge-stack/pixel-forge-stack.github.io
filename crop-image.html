The Basics

Cropping Removes Pixels, Resizing Rescales Them

To crop is to cut away the outer parts of a picture so that only a chosen rectangle remains. The pixels inside that rectangle are not rescaled: a 600 × 400 crop of a 4000 × 3000 photo is a 600 × 400 image with the original detail.

Resizing is different. It keeps the whole picture and changes its pixel dimensions, which is what Resize Image does. Often you want both: crop to the composition and ratio you want, then resize to the dimensions you need.

Composition

Why Crop?

Remove distractions. Cut away edges, bystanders or clutter that draw attention from the subject.Improve composition. Moving the subject off-centre, cutting closer or leaving space on one side can change how a picture feels. Cropping tight on a small subject also magnifies flaws, so it works best when the original has plenty of pixels.Change the shape. Portrait, landscape and square frames suit different uses. Cropping lets you choose the shape instead of stretching the picture into it.
Aspect Ratio

Choosing a Ratio

The ratio buttons lock the shape of the selection: 1:1 (square), 4:3, 3:2, 5:4, 16:9 (widescreen), and the portrait versions 9:16 and 2:3. "Free" lets you drag any rectangle.

Platforms often prefer particular shapes, for example square profile pictures, wide banners or tall vertical video. Their exact recommendations change over time, so check the platform's current guidance. Crop first, then resize to a specific size with tools such as Instagram, YouTube thumbnail or LinkedIn banner. If you would rather keep the whole picture and add space, use Change Aspect Ratio.

How It Works

Using the Crop Tool

Open an image, drag on it to select an area, adjust the corner handles, or type exact X, Y, width and height values in pixels. Choose JPG, PNG or WebP and download. The crop is saved at the original pixel resolution, and your file is not changed.

The tool crops one image at a time. When saving as JPG, transparent areas are not given a background colour, so pick PNG or WebP for transparent images.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between cropping and resizing? ▾
Cropping removes part of the picture and keeps the pixels that remain at their original size. Resizing keeps the whole picture and changes how many pixels it has. A cropped image is smaller in dimensions only because pixels were cut away.
Does cropping reduce the quality? ▾
Not by itself. The kept area keeps its original pixel detail. Saving as JPG or WebP applies compression, so choose PNG if you want to avoid that.
How do I crop to an exact size? ▾
Use the manual coordinates (X, Y, width and height in pixels) to define the area. To end up with a specific final size, crop to the right ratio and then use Resize Image.
Will cropping make the file smaller? ▾
Often, because fewer pixels are saved. The exact change depends on the content and format. For a specific file-size target, see Compress Image.
Why is the background black when I save a transparent PNG as JPG? ▾
JPG cannot store transparency, and this tool does not add a background colour. Choose PNG or WebP to keep transparency.
Does the original file change? ▾
No. The tool saves a new file and leaves your original untouched.
